# Global AI Developments and Their Diffusion to Türkiye — Landscape, Classification and Opportunities (snapshot 3 October 2026)

## 1. Executive summary

This report scans 73 AI-driven developments (48 across all major sectors, 25 in ecology, research and civil society), scores each on six diffusion drivers, and classifies each for two horizons, 2026–2028 and 2028–2031, applying a fixed rule to the scores rather than assigning classes by judgement. Evidence was gathered on 3 October 2026 from a register of 682 source records (376 of them cited in this report). Two Opus review passes, which between them covered every row, challenged the classifications before they were finalised, and the resulting data files accompany this report.

The headline is that AI is reaching Türkiye through three channels of very different reliability. The most reliable is binding regulation, and almost all of it is imported: the EU AI Act reaches Turkish exporters and EU-facing providers (A39, A29, A50), and EU-harmonised or sectoral rules do the same in medical devices and banking (A02, A45). No Turkish AI-systems law exists. The second channel is the state deploying where it owns both the data and the platform: tax-risk triage (A12), national crop monitoring (A14) and a student assistant with roughly 719,000 users (A08) already run at scale, while citizen-facing services that cut across agencies are mostly announcements (A03, A11, A32, A48). The third channel is funding, and it is what carries ecology: six biodiversity-monitoring developments are classed Likely (B01, B02, B03, B06, B17, B23), but four of them depend on a single Biodiversa+ call stream open to Turkish teams (S108), and operational use in Türkiye is still largely absent.

Of the 73 developments, 17 are Certain in both horizons, 8 are Likely in 2026–2028 rising to 16 in 2028–2031, and the rest are Potential. No development was classed low-probability; this reflects the rule, which required a sourced structural barrier, not proof that barriers do not exist. Half of the 2028–2031 Likely classes (8 of 16) rest on an assumed lag behind comparable economies rather than on Turkish evidence and should be read as indicative.

Two patterns matter most for a reader deciding what to do. First, governance arrives before deployment: rules for generative AI in research and universities are already in force (B14, A09) while the tools they govern are still Potential (B10, B11, B13, B25), so anything built now must fit existing disclosure and verification duties. Second, in 12 of the 17 open Tier B developments the deciding condition is not technology or data but a named Turkish institution funding a real deployment. Announcement volume overstates Turkish deployment: four developments were downgraded during review because only a label, an offline study, an unverified figure or a single self-report stood behind the "AI" claim (A47, A25, A27, A46).

For the researcher profile in Section 7 the synthesis recommends three directions, in order: AI-supported monitoring of wetland and delta restoration with the Gediz Delta as demonstrator; institution-facing evidence synthesis run on verified multi-agent pipelines; and municipal nature data combining citizen science with satellite mapping, with the metropolitan municipality as first client. The first step of each can be taken within 90 days and is specified in Section 6.

## 2. Method and limitations

**Workflow.** The study used a multi-model pattern. A planner agent fixed the brief, scoring rules and rule clarifications, and logged its decisions. Sonnet agents ran the scans and drafted Sections 2–5 and 7; the planner wrote Sections 1 and 6. Opus agents reviewed inventory coverage before scanning, checked calibration items and challenged classifications in two passes. Haiku agents merged files, ran structural source checks and applied class-neutral fixes, all logged in `STATE.md` and `review_log.md`.

**Inventory.** Initial scans gave 43 Tier A and 22 Tier B items; an Opus coverage review added, merged and split items, giving 73 rows (Tier A 48, Tier B 25). Tourism is excluded because the brief's scope does not name it.

**Scoring.** Each development is scored 1–5 on six drivers: regulatory pull (d1), funding pull (d2), cost pressure (d3), infrastructure and data readiness (d4), workforce readiness (d5) and existing local activity (d6). Fixed anchors apply. For d1, 5 is a Turkish binding law with a dated obligation, 4 is binding foreign law reaching a material share of Turkish actors (or binding Turkish secondary regulation), 3 is a target, draft or permissive approval, 2 is soft guidance and 1 is none. For d2, 5 is a dedicated call with budget for the item, 4 is a named programme (edition in the last 24 months) whose scope names the application, and 3 is generic funding. For d3, 4 is a sourced saving of 25–50%; vendor claims alone stay at 3. For d6, 4 is presence beyond pilot in several Turkish organisations (or a primary national survey showing at least 10% use), 3 is one funded pilot or an active group, and 2 is isolated mentions. Scores must evidence the AI component itself.

**Classification rule.** First match wins. (1) Certain if d1 ≥ 4, or d6 ≥ 4, or (d6 ≥ 3 and a funded operational pilot in Türkiye and (d2 ≥ 3 or d3 ≥ 4)). (2) Low probability / delayed if a sourced barrier exists, or min(d4, d5) ≤ 1, or mean(d4, d5) < 2.0. (3) Likely if (d2 ≥ 4 or d3 ≥ 4), at least two sourced analogues, and mean(d4, d5) ≥ 2.5. (4) Potential otherwise, with one named deciding condition; rows with two or more analogues are flagged near-Likely. A funded pilot is an operational deployment with users shown by a primary source; R&D, trainings and strategies do not count.

**Horizons.** The 1–2 year horizon is 2026–2028 and the 3–5 year horizon is 2028–2031. The second re-runs the rule after only three dated adjustments: d1 rises to the level of any obligation applying in 2028–2031; d6 rises by one if there are at least two analogues (an analogue-to-Türkiye lag assumption); a barrier clears only if a source dates its removal. A 3–5 year Certain that rests only on the assumed d6 rise is reported as Likely. This addition, permitted by the brief, is labelled **Likely (lag-based)** and applies to eight items (A11, A16, A32, A41, A42, A44, A46, B27). Rows tagged **GOVERNANCE** (A09, A29, A39, A45, B14) are rules in place, not deployed technologies, so Certain means the rule binds or applies.

**Review.** The first Opus pass covered 36 rows: 27 agreed, 4 disagreed and 5 stayed open. The disagreements (A15, A46, B01, B17) were resolved by planner rulings with logged reasons, and open items by targeted searches. The second pass covered 43 re-run rows: 38 agreed, 4 disagreed (A10, A25, A47, B09) and 1 stayed open (A27).

**Limitations.**

- *Depth.* Only abstracts, summaries and landing pages were read, by design.
- *Search-only sourcing.* Evidence comes from web-search results and Consensus/Scholar abstracts. A session cap of 200 searches degraded batches b1 and b3–b9, which were partly re-run; Turkish grey literature is under-detected, so d6 = 1–2 and analogue_count = 0 often mean 'not found' rather than 'absent' (explicitly B11, B13, A18). This is a structured snapshot, not a systematic review.
- *Analogue counting.* Plans, trainings and announced pilots were excluded after review, and some affiliations were inferred from titles (B23, B27), so analogue counts are uneven.
- *Calls named in Section 6.* Whether TÜBİTAK participates in the 2026–27 Biodiversa+ BiodivFuture call (S675, S676) was not verified, and whether that call carries a monitoring or restoration-success topic was not checked.
- *Source checking.* Outbound HTTP was blocked, so a URL counts as verified when it appeared in a search result and passed a structural check, not when fetched. Of 682 source rows, 607 passed the structural check (7 of these are orphans kept for inventory-stage provenance and 7 are flagged "URL via search result"), 74 are labelled weak (secondary, vendor or summary-level) and 1 is a duplicate of S500. Weak sources carry "(secondary source)" in Section 7. Section 7 lists only the 376 sources cited in this text; the full register is in `sources.csv`.
- *Lag assumption.* The +1 on d6 is an assumption, not an observation, and it produces the eight lag-based classes.
- *Scope reading of S108.* The d2 = 4 scores for B01, B04, B17 and B23 treat monitoring as the core activity of a Biodiversa+ call (S108) that names no technology. A strict reading would move B01, B17 and B23 to Potential (near-Likely). B06 also rests on S108, but on the call's named topic of measuring restoration success, so it does not depend on the monitoring-core reading.
- *Survey-share rule.* A10 reaches Certain on a primary survey figure (S672). The comparable A27 figure could not be verified and was scored at d6 = 2.
- *No item reached Low.* Every row has barrier = none, because no search sourced a barrier, and min(d4, d5) ≥ 2 throughout. A21 and B25 sit exactly at a mean of 2.0. In almost every row (72 of 73 by a text search of the notes) at least one score is flagged as an unsourced estimate. The rule may therefore under-detect Low outcomes.
- *Absence of evidence.* "None found" for Türkiye is not proof of absence; several Turkish claims rest on press snippets. EU Omnibus dates come from law-firm digests, not the Official Journal. The snapshot date is 2026-10-03, and all statements are calibrated estimates.

### 3.2 Sector narratives

**Health (A01–A04, A49).** The one Certain item is A02, AI-enabled imaging devices. It rests on a binding route (Türkiye's medical-device regulation is harmonised with EU MDR, so AI imaging software needs registration [S122]) and on deployment: Hevi AI reports 35 Turkish hospitals [S118], and TÜSEB signed a cooperation protocol for public-hospital rollout in November 2025 [S119]. Leaders report strong effects, such as a 44.3% lower reading workload in the MASAI trial [S121]. Workflow tools remain Potential. A01 ambient scribes are scaled in the USA (62.6% of Epic hospitals [S060]) and live at Korea's Asan Medical Center [S063], but only vendor dictation offers were found in Türkiye [S066]. A03 voice agents show vendor offers only [S130]. A04 drug discovery is R&D, such as the TÜBİTAK-supported DrugGEN [S608]. A49 health-data reuse has no Turkish regime yet; the Action Plan only targets a data library [S133]. Regulated products appear to diffuse first.

**Finance (A05, A06, A07, A45, A46).** A45 is Certain and GOVERNANCE-tagged: BDDK's remote-identification rule requires liveness checks [S174, secondary source], and video onboarding has been routine since 2021 [S175; S176]. A06 credit scoring is Certain at medium confidence, because press and column sources describe machine-learning scoring as routine [S160; S162], while alternative data is evidenced mainly by KKB Web Score [S160]. A46 insurance was downgraded to Potential (Likely at 3–5 years): only Aksigorta self-reports AI claims handling [S187], TARSİM's satellite article does not state AI [S185], and the Anadolu Sigorta item is undated [S186]. A05 agentic fraud and A07 agentic payments stay Potential. Garanti BBVA uses machine-learning fraud scoring [S157], but agentic payments are live only in Brazil and Mexico [S165; S166], and Türkiye shows announcements [S172].

**Education (A08–A10).** All three are Certain, for different reasons. A08 rests on the MEB platform whose KANKA assistant was used by 719,479 students [S609; S610]. A09 is GOVERNANCE-tagged: YÖK's ethics guide [S416] and university rules covering exams and assessment [S614; S615] are in place, and YÖK says further legislation is in preparation [S613]. A10 reaches Certain through the survey-share rule: OECD TALIS 2024 reports 24% of Turkish lower-secondary teachers using AI, against 43% in Korea and 56% in Brazil [S672; S673; S674]. Pedagogical evidence is mixed. A Harvard trial found an AI tutor beat in-class active learning [S139], while unguarded access harmed learning in another study [S143], so design may matter more than access.

**Public administration (A11–A13, A48).** A12, case triage, is Certain: Treasury and tax-board pages indicate the KURGAN risk-analysis system has been operational since October 2025 [S564; S565], with press reporting 17,373 sellers screened [S566]. Evidence covers tax only, not benefits or permits. A11 assistants are Potential at 1–2 years and Likely (lag-based) at 3–5: Poland's mObywatel assistant went live on PLLuM [S217], while Türkiye has targets [S133], staff training [S221] and a municipal call-routing system [S223], but no e-Devlet assistant. A13 agentic workflows remain at procurement stage in Korea [S218], with none found in Türkiye. A48 disaster damage assessment relies on future-tense AFAD announcements [S657; S658] and earthquake research [S245; S246].

**Agriculture (A14–A16, A47).** A14 is Certain: the ministry's AI-assisted Ürün İzleme Sistemi has been in use since the 2025 production year, with yield forecasts starting in four provinces [S264; S265]. A47 irrigation was downgraded to Potential because the ministry page describes sensors and a cloud platform, but no AI function [S284]. A15 advisory chatbots are Potential (near-Likely): the FAO's USD 30 to 3 per-farmer figure [S272] has unconfirmed attribution, and Türkiye shows only commercial offers [S275]. A16 precision spraying is at prototype stage [S281; S282] and is Likely (lag-based) at 3–5 years. IPARD funding is generic [S269; S270].

**Energy (A17–A19).** A17 grid forecasting is Likely at both horizons: Horizon Europe lists AI-for-grid topics [S293], and Korea (KEPCO) and Poland (Enea) are analogues [S297; S298]. Turkish evidence is pilot-level, such as TÜBİTAK's wind-forecast centre [S295] and an EPDK-supported disaster-damage tool tested in Marmara [S296]. A18 data-centre flexibility rests on a US field demonstration [S301] and has no Turkish rule or analogue. A19 building optimisation shows field savings of 13–16% in reviews [S304] and a Suwon commercial-building study [S305]. Türkiye offers generic KOSGEB support [S627] and a press headline without figures [S628]. Both are Potential, so a funded Turkish deployment would change the picture.

**Manufacturing (A20–A22).** A20 industrial robots is Certain on deployment (26,413 robots in the 2023 stock [S311]; 171 robots at Arçelik's Ankara plant [S324]) and the HIT-30 call [S312]. The AI share of that robot stock is unsourced, so the label fits robotics more than AI specifically. A22 predictive maintenance and inspection is Certain through the funded-pilot clause (Arçelik's AI-supported plant [S325]; Aslıteks' TÜBİTAK-supported fabric inspection [S326]). A21 humanoids is Potential: Turkish activity is producer-side [S320; S321], and the IFR does not expect near-term mass adoption [S317].

**Logistics (A23–A25).** All three are Potential. A23 warehouse robotics has Polish [S629; S630] and Korean [S322] analogues, but Türkiye shows only a corporate statement about Trendyol Operations [S631], and simulated savings of 14.5–24.1% [S334] fall below the 25% anchor. A24 autonomous trucking has a Ford Trucks Level-4 prototype tested with a safety driver on a closed section [S072; S073] and an enabling type-approval regulation of December 2024 [S075]. A25 route optimisation was downgraded: the Getir deep-reinforcement-learning study [S632] was an offline evaluation, not a live deployment, per the review log. The item may be understated.

**Media (A26–A29).** A29 provenance and labelling is Certain and GOVERNANCE-tagged: AI Act Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026 [S347; S350] and reaches Turkish providers serving EU users [S079; S089]. A Digital Omnibus grace period for some marking duties could soften this [S112; S113]. A27 answer engines was downgraded because the 14% Turkish chatbot-news figure [S638] could not be verified against the primary report [S639]. A26 content production has a US RCT showing 40% less writing time [S339], but isolated Turkish mentions [S633]. A28 licensing has a Brazilian precedent [S640] and no Turkish AI-training rule [S641].

**Law (A30, A32).** A30 legal drafting is Potential: leaders include Harvey [S043] and an RCT with 50–130% productivity gains on five of six tasks [S204]. Turkish evidence is a vendor, Leagle [S661], and press reports [S662], and Brazil is the only counted analogue [S663]. Polish and Korean firm cases were not counted because their sources could not be attributed, so a verified second analogue could lift the item to Likely. A32 court AI is Potential, with Likely (lag-based) at 3–5 years. The Justice Ministry announced a UYAP AI decision-support system in May 2026 [S667; S668], but operation is unconfirmed, while Brazil [S211; S212], Mexico [S210] and Korea [S669] serve as analogues.

**Retail (A34, A35).** A34 personalisation is Certain on company-reported use at Trendyol and Hepsiburada [S192; S194] and Migros [S193], at medium confidence. Dynamic pricing is thinner, and the competition authority monitors algorithmic pricing [S195; S196], which restricts rather than pulls adoption. A35 customer-service agents are Potential: Garanti BBVA's assistant serves 8.2 million customers [S643] and Trendyol runs seller agents [S671], but the generative component is unconfirmed and no analogue deployment was found. Chinese field experiments [S201; S202] and a US study of 5,172 agents [S200] show effects elsewhere, which may transfer if Turkish deployments are verified.

**Labour market (A36–A38, A50, A51).** A36 new AI roles is Certain: AI/ML vacancies are listed [S377], though AI skills are only 0.36% of LinkedIn members [S354]. A50 AI literacy is Certain through the EU AI Act Article 4 duty, which the Omnibus recast but kept [S618; S619]. A37 clerical decline, A38 AI freelancing and A51 hiring algorithms stay Potential. Exposure estimates are not observed decline: 41% of Turkish employment is highly exposed [S354]. A51 depends on the AI Act high-risk date of 2 December 2027 [S366; S369].

**Cross-cutting (A39–A42, A44).** A39 AI Act obligations is Certain and GOVERNANCE-tagged: it reaches Turkish providers [S079; S089], while Türkiye has no AI-systems law in force [S090]. A40 Turkish-language LLMs is Likely on d2 = 4: a sectoral adaptation grant of up to 50 million TL per project [S567; S568], plus analogues in Poland [S234], Korea [S236] and Indonesia [S237]. BİLGE is announced but not yet a public service [S230; S231]. A41 agents, A42 compute and A44 cyber are Potential, and Likely (lag-based) at 3–5 years. Law 7545 sets incident duties but does not mandate AI detection [S651].

## 4. Tier B deep focus

Each item below gives the evidence from leaders and diffusion analogues, named examples, the Türkiye status, the classification per horizon with the rule branch (C1 Certain, C2 Low, C3 Likely, C4 Potential), and the condition that decides the outcome. "Analogue" means one of Poland, Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia or South Korea.

### 4.1 Ecology and environment (B01–B07, B09, B23, B24)

**B01 – Passive acoustic monitoring with AI species classifiers**
*Evidence.* Autonomous recorders paired with classifiers are scaled in research and agency practice. BirdNET [S095], Google DeepMind's Perch [S013] and PNW-Cnet [S016] are the named tools, and European tests suggest BirdNET can approach expert performance for birds [S091; S092]. Brazil (Pantanal [S093]) and Poland (Poznań [S094]) are analogues, both academic fieldwork. A dedicated EU network, TABMON, exists [S109]. Savings are context-dependent: acoustic surveys beat point counts but not for small surveys [S097]. *Türkiye.* No Turkish passive-acoustic AI project was found in four searches, only a TRDizin paper on deep-learning recognition of Aras bird species from sound [S500]; this is absence of evidence, not proof. *Classification.* Likely at both horizons (C3: d2 = 4, two analogues, mean d4/d5 = 3.5). The d2 score rests on the Biodiversa+ BiodivConnect call, open to Turkish applicants through TÜBİTAK [S108]. *Deciding condition.* The class would reverse to Potential (near-Likely) if a 2026–27 Biodiversa+ edition no longer carries a monitoring topic open to Türkiye. A funded Turkish acoustic-AI field project would instead lift it to Certain.

**B02 – Camera-trap image analysis with AI detectors and species classifiers**

### 4.2 Academia and research (B10–B15, B25)

**B10 – AI research assistants for literature discovery and synthesis**
*Evidence.* Elicit, Consensus and similar tools are used commercially in the USA and EU, but an independent test found Elicit search sensitivity of about 39.5% against 94.5% for conventional searching, so they are an adjunct only [S409]. EKUAL and a university database notice list Scopus and Web of Science without an AI add-on [S573; S574], and YÖK's guide is soft guidance [S416]. *Classification.* Potential at both horizons (C4): d6 = 3, d3 = 3, one analogue. *Deciding condition.* A TÜBİTAK/EKUAL- or university-funded licence for AI research assistants, with measured time savings in Turkish institutions (d6 ≥ 4 or d3 ≥ 4), would change the class; a confirmed AI add-on in EKUAL would make it Certain. Absence in search snippets is not proof of absence.

**B11 – LLM-assisted screening and data extraction in systematic reviews**
*Evidence.* A 63-study review reports median screening agreement of 0.92 and 0.89 and median extraction accuracy of 0.95, but with a range from 0.36 to 1.00, so cautious implementation is advised [S017]. Named results include Elicit extraction at F1 92% [S410], Claude 2 extraction at 96.3% accuracy [S014] and GPT-4 prompt templates at 97.7% sensitivity [S405]. Workload reductions of 33–93% [S406] and costs of USD 157 against more than USD 1,667 per 10,000 citations [S405] support d3 = 4, but outcome-data extraction was error-free in only 27.8–80.7% of cases [S408]. *Türkiye.* No Turkish LLM-screening study or deployment was found; EKUAL offers EBSCO AI trainings [S414] and generic generative-AI use is documented [S403]. No analogue application was sourced, which is a gap and not evidence of absence. *Classification.* Potential at both horizons (C4): d3 = 4, but zero analogues blocks C3. *Deciding condition.* Sourced in-country screening by research groups in at least two analogue economies would give Likely. This is the highest-value follow-up in this domain.

**B12 – AI-maintained living evidence databases for conservation decisions**
*Evidence.* The Cambridge-based Conservation Evidence database [S432] and its Evidence Pipeline, which screened about 250 million papers, are the main case [S433]. No analogue deployment was found. *Türkiye.* No Turkish AI-maintained evidence database was found in three searches. İş Bankası's Büyük Doğa Modeli Türkiye links park recordings to academic studies but is an art and data project, not evidence synthesis [S597]. Biodiversa+ calls remain topic-adjacent [S108; S096]. *Classification.* Potential at both horizons (C4): d2 = 3, d3 = 3, d6 = 2, zero analogues. Content and tools are English-language, which may slow uptake.

**B13 – Agentic coding assistants for researcher data analysis**
*Evidence.* Anthropic launched a Claude Science workbench in desktop beta on 30 June 2026 [S030; S435], and the Innovations for Poverty Action documents Claude Code use cases [S436]. Independent benchmarking suggests agents remain unreliable: in ResearchGym a GPT-5 agent beat baselines in one of 15 runs [S438]. Adoption among scientists was about 2% (secondary estimate [S437]). No analogue use by in-country research institutions was found. *Türkiye.* No institutional licence or use of agentic coding tools was found. The TRUBA ARF-ACC supercomputer provides compute to academia [S598], Horizon Europe's RAISE call funds AI for science generally [S605; S606], and YÖK's guide requires disclosure [S416]. **B14 – Publisher, funder and university rules on generative AI in research** (GOVERNANCE)
*Evidence.* Elsevier [S039] and Springer Nature [S420] restrict AI use in manuscripts and review, the NIH bars AI in peer review [S421], Horizon Europe excludes AI from proposal evaluation [S422], and the European Research Area guidelines were revised in May 2026 [S044]. Poland (NCN, May 2025 [S423]), Brazil (CNPq Ordinance 2.664/2026, secondary [S424]) and South Korea (NRF/ministry guide, secondary [S425]) are analogues. *Türkiye.* YÖK approved its ethics guide on 18 April 2024 [S416]; universities have issued guides [S417; S418], journals have AI policies [S419], and TÜBİTAK requires disclosure and bans AI use by evaluators [S100; S101]. It would reverse if policy existence were not read as presence, because compliance is uneven and an ethics-training gap is reported [S404].

**B15 – Research-integrity screening for paper mills and fabricated content**

**B25 – Multi-agent and autonomous AI research systems ("AI co-scientists")**

## 5. Cross-cutting patterns

The classifications suggest seven patterns in how AI developments reach Türkiye. They describe this inventory and are not causal findings.

**1. Regulation reaches Türkiye through exporters and product markets.** The clearest Certain classes rest on foreign binding law reaching Turkish firms, not on Turkish AI law: the EU AI Act (A39, A29, A50) and EU-aligned medical-device rules (A02). Türkiye has no AI-systems law in force [S090]. The route is fragile: the Digital Omnibus softened some duties and moved high-risk dates [S112; S113], and the EU Machinery Regulation, applicable from 20 January 2027, reaches robot makers more than adopters (A20, A23) [S315; S316, secondary source].

**2. Operational public platforms are the clearest public-sector signal.** Where the state deploys, it does so on platforms it owns: the MEB assistant (A08), the KURGAN tax-triage system (A12), the Ürün İzleme Sistemi (A14) and, at municipal level, Melikgazi's assistant (B20). Certain items on the private side (A06, A20, A22, A34) rest more often on company or press reports. Where the evidence was only an "AI-supported" label (A47) or an offline study (A25), the review downgraded the item.

**3. Funding channels pull specific items.** The sectoral Turkish-LLM adaptation call (up to 50 million TL per project [S567; S568]) gives A40 its d2 = 4, while TÜBİTAK 1007 Kamu YZ Ekosistemi [S588; S589] lifts B22, B26 and B27 only to d2 = 3. Biodiversa+ underpins B01, B17, B06 and B23, but through a single call (S108), a dependency to watch. Horizon Europe association [S098] supports A17 [S293] and A24 [S110], and Sivil Düşün supports B21 and B26 [S582; S583].

**4. Governance precedes deployment.** Rules are in place before the technology: YÖK and TÜBİTAK rules on generative AI (B14, A09), BDDK's liveness requirement (A45) and the type-approval regulation for automated vehicles with no operating driverless truck (A24). Five items carry the GOVERNANCE tag, and they show where rules, not tools, are diffusing.

**5. Turkish-language capability is a recurring gate, not yet a barrier.** Deciding conditions for A01, A03, A11, B22 and B26 name Turkish-language validation. A40 shows national investment, with BİLGE announced but not yet public [S230; S231]. No row recorded a sourced language barrier, so the gate is a condition on timing.

**6. Municipalities are the most active civil-society adopters.** İzmir, Nilüfer, Melikgazi, Bağcılar and Kahramanmaraş appear in B19, B20, B26 and B27, while NGO-side items (B18, B21) score lower on local activity. University and NGO groups lead only in monitoring (B04, B17).

**7. The analogue lag matters, and is an assumption.** Eight items become Likely at 3–5 years only through the lag rule (A11, A16, A32, A41, A42, A44, A46, B27). Korea and Poland supply most analogues, and the lag may be slower than assumed where Turkish validation or funding is missing.

Across the 73 rows, 17 are Certain at both horizons, Likely rises from 8 to 16 items between 1–2 and 3–5 years, and Potential falls from 48 to 40. None is Low. Most Potential items lack a funded Turkish operational pilot, which is the condition most often named as decisive.

## 6. Synthesis

### 6.1 General landscape

The classification pattern says more about Türkiye than about AI. Technology and data are rarely the binding constraint: the infrastructure-and-data driver (d4) sits at 3 or 4 for most developments, and no row produced a sourced structural barrier. What separates Certain from Potential is almost always an institution. Most binding pull comes from EU law or EU-harmonised rules (A39, A29, A50, A02); the main domestic exception is a sectoral banking rule (A45). Where a platform runs at national scale, a ministry owns the data; where a Likely class appears without Turkish deployment, a funding programme is doing the work. The state has moved fast where it controls the whole stack (KURGAN went into production in October 2025; MEBİ's KANKA assistant was used by about 719,000 students [S610]), while services that need several agencies or external providers are still mostly announced (A03, A11, A32, A48).

Civil society follows the same logic one level down. Most documented civil-society AI activity in the inventory is municipal: Melikgazi's assistant (B20), İzmir's staff training (B19), Bağcılar's İSTKA-supported twin (B27) and Kahramanmaraş's call routing (B26). The NGO side is thin (B18 none found, B21 one adjacent volunteer-matching service, B22 none found). This may partly reflect what is documented online rather than what NGOs do, which is why a single documented, funded use would count for a lot.

Ecology is the clearest case of funding-led diffusion. The six Likely monitoring developments owe their class to Biodiversa+ eligibility and to cost evidence from peer-reviewed work abroad, not to Turkish practice. The Türkiye baseline is a handful of research pilots (camera-trap detection at İYTE [S382], a national fish eDNA survey at Ankara University [S390], a TÜBİTAK-funded lagoon eDNA project at Boğaziçi [S391] and a TRDizin paper on Aras bird calls [S500]) and one international training on the Gediz Delta (S501). The gap between "eligible and cheap" and "operational and funded" is exactly the space a connected researcher can occupy, and it is where the three directions below sit.

### 6.2 Three recommended directions

The researcher's advantages, as the inventory reveals them, are specific: field expertise in wetlands, herpetofauna, Odonata and vegetation that can produce the ground truth that Turkish wetland-mapping studies report as scarce (S401); partners that already appear in the evidence (Tour du Valat's Mediterranean Wetlands Observatory trained Ege University and Doğa Derneği on the Gediz Delta, S501; the Gediz wet-meadow restoration monitors soil and water with no AI or satellite layer, S525); a municipal partner at a time when municipalities are the only civil-society actors deploying; and production-grade multi-agent Claude Code workflows that stand in for a coding team. The advice that follows from this is to position as the person who validates, operationalises and synthesises, not the one who builds models. Two tempting directions were rejected on that basis. The first is nature finance: corporate nature disclosure (B07) waits for a binding Turkish or EU-buyer duty, and biodiversity credits (B24) for a Turkish legal basis and MRV protocol, with no analogue market yet; neither is something the researcher can create, so both are watch items for 2028–2031 rather than directions (they would reopen if TSRS added a nature duty or the ministry recognised nature credits). The second is building EO foundation models or "AI co-scientist" systems (B05, B25), which needs ML engineering capacity the profile does not have and adds less value than being their validated user.

**Direction 1. AI-supported monitoring of wetland and delta restoration, with the Gediz Delta as demonstrator.**
Why: digital measurement of restoration outcomes (B06) is Likely in both horizons and fits the Biodiversa+ call text better than any other item ("setting restoration targets and measuring success", S108). Drone habitat mapping (B23) and acoustic monitoring (B01) are Likely. Satellite wetland mapping (B04) is Potential, and its deciding condition, a funded operational wetland-monitoring service in Türkiye, is one the researcher's own network can create. No operational, field-validated AI monitoring product for a Turkish delta was found in the searches (B04, B06).
When: 2026–2028 through consortium funding, triggered by a Biodiversa+ edition open to Turkish teams or a Horizon Europe Earth-observation topic (S514 lists Türkiye as eligible for the 2026 Cluster 4 EO topics, per search summary). In 2028–2031 it becomes an operational service if the site management body or the metropolitan municipality adopts it, which would move B04 to Certain.
First step, next 90 days: this week, check whether TÜBİTAK participates in the Biodiversa+ "Novel ecosystems" (BiodivFuture) call, launched 9 September 2026 with pre-proposals due in early November 2026 (S675, S676; participation unconfirmed at the time of writing). A delta reshaped by salt pans, drainage and restoration is a defensible novel ecosystem. If Türkiye participates, join or lead a pre-proposal with Tour du Valat as EU partner and the national nature NGO as stakeholder partner. Whatever the call outcome, build a 2017–2026 Sentinel-1/2 wetland-extent and condition time series for the Gediz Delta using MWO tooling or Google Earth Engine driven by a Claude Code pipeline, validate it against the researcher's own vegetation plots and Odonata and herpetofauna sites, and offer it to the wet-meadow restoration team as the missing outcome layer.
Main risk: single-call dependency. Four Likely classes rest on one reading of the Biodiversa+ call text, and Türkiye has no Nature Restoration Law duty (B09). Note that the 2026–27 BiodivFuture call is themed on novel ecosystems; whether it names monitoring or restoration success, and whether TÜBİTAK takes part, was not verified (S675, S676). If it does neither, the deciding condition for B01, B06, B17 and B23 is not met for this edition and those classes should be read as Potential (near-Likely) until the next call. Hedge by designing one indicator set that serves three buyers at once, namely national CBD/GBF and Ramsar reporting (B09), the municipality's adaptation plans (B27) and restoration MRV (B06), and by pursuing a generic TÜBİTAK research grant (e.g. 1001; not assessed in this study) or a Horizon EO topic (S514) in parallel. The technical risk, class confusion among saltmarsh, reed and salt-pan habitats, is exactly what the researcher's field validation addresses, and that validation is the selling point.

**Direction 2. Institution-facing evidence synthesis run on verified multi-agent pipelines.**
Why: LLM-assisted screening and extraction (B11) has sourced cost evidence (d3 = 4: screening workload cut by 33–93% [S406], though extraction is less reliable [S408]) and is Potential only because analogue use was not detected. AI-maintained living evidence for conservation decisions (B12) is Potential with the deciding condition "a funded operational Turkish deployment", which a single funded pilot by the researcher would satisfy, moving it to Certain under the rule (mechanically, through the C1 third clause, and only if a primary source documents users; one pilot would not by itself show national diffusion). Evidence-to-policy briefs (B22) need Turkish-language validation. The governance frame is already fixed (B14 Certain; TÜBİTAK and YÖK disclosure and verification rules, S100, S101, S416), so the compliance cost of starting now is known. This report is itself a worked example of the method.
When: now. This direction does not wait for diffusion because the researcher is the adopter. Scale depends on a public client with funding, for example the next TÜBİTAK 1007 Kamu YZ Ekosistemi edition, whose 2024 and 2026 rounds both carried a climate and sustainability theme (S588, S589) but require a public customer institution (whether a metropolitan municipality qualifies was not checked); Sivil Düşün (S582) can fund the NGO or cooperative side.
First step, next 90 days: deliver one demonstrator synthesis for a named partner, WWF-Türkiye or the national nature NGO, on a question they already have, such as the effectiveness of wetland and wet-meadow restoration interventions in Mediterranean deltas or amphibian and Odonata responses to pond creation and hydrological restoration. Write it to Conservation Evidence conventions with a Turkish policy brief. Build in a measured verification protocol, a manual double-check of at least 20% of screening and extraction decisions with reported accuracy and hours saved, and publish that protocol as a short methods note. This creates the Turkish evidence for B11 and B12 that the scan could not find.
Main risk: commoditisation, since Elicit, Consensus and Scopus AI are cheap and spreading (B10). Hedge by selling domain judgement, Turkish-language output and an audit trail rather than search. The second risk is credibility loss from fabricated citations or extraction errors (B15). Hedge with human-verified extraction, a disclosed AI-use statement under the TÜBİTAK and YÖK rules, and one review of the pipeline by a co-author who reads code.

**Direction 3. Municipal nature data combining citizen science and satellite mapping, with the metropolitan municipality as first client and the cooperative as operator.**
Why: AI-assisted citizen-science species identification (B17) is Likely in both horizons, yet Turkish activity is event-scale (S451). In our judgement (not sourced in the inventory), expert validators for difficult taxa remain the bottleneck that automated identification does not remove, and Odonata and herpetofauna are such taxa. Urban-nature and climate-adaptation mapping for municipalities (B27) is Potential now and Likely later, with a deciding condition that names the researcher's partner type: a funded metropolitan project that operationalises canopy, heat or blue-green mapping. Municipalities are training staff but lack operational tools (B19, B26).
When: 2026–2028 for the citizen-science layer, which can start on existing partner resources; 2028–2031 for an operational municipal service. Triggers are a municipal adaptation or EU Cities Mission commitment (İzmir is a Cities Mission city, S591, if the partner is İzmir Büyükşehir Belediyesi), the next TÜBİTAK 1007 edition, or a development-agency AI programme on the model of the İSTKA support for Bağcılar's digital twin (S593); an equivalent İZKA programme is an unsourced estimate.
First step, next 90 days: agree with the municipality's environment or climate department a one-season urban blue-green biodiversity baseline for three to five urban wetlands, streams or parks. The cooperative runs public iNaturalist bioblitz events under one umbrella project with the researcher as expert validator for Odonata and herpetofauna; pair the observations with a Sentinel-2 change map of green and blue cover built in the same pipeline as Direction 1; and sign a short data protocol with an open licence and municipal use rights before the first event.
Main risk: municipal budget and political cycles, and dependence on a single department. Hedge with a memorandum that includes an open-data clause so the dataset survives staff turnover, and a co-applicant NGO so the work can move to Sivil Düşün or Biodiversa+ funding. The accuracy of automated identification for Turkish Odonata and herpetofauna is a second risk; reporting expert-validated accuracy turns it into a publishable result.

**Why this order.** Direction 1 ranks first because the researcher's comparative advantage, field ground truth on a delta the partners already work on, is largest there, and the inventory shows funding pull already in place (B06 Likely, S108). Direction 2 ranks above Direction 3 because it needs no external funding or partner approval, produces the first Turkish deployment evidence within weeks, and its verification protocol is reused by the other two. Direction 3 depends on a municipal budget cycle and a department's agreement, so it is the anchor, not the engine. The ranking reflects expected value, not start order: start Direction 2 first in time and give Direction 1 the most effort.

### 6.3 How the three fit together

The three directions share one pipeline and one validation asset. The Sentinel time series built for the Gediz Delta in Direction 1 is the same change map Direction 3 needs for urban blue-green cover; the verification protocol of Direction 2 is what makes the monitoring outputs of Directions 1 and 3 citable by institutions. Direction 2 can start this month with no external funding and produces the first Turkish deployment evidence; Direction 1 is the funding play and should be timed to the Biodiversa+ pre-proposal window; Direction 3 is the lowest-cost, highest-visibility local anchor. If only one can be pursued, Direction 1 is the choice, because it is where the researcher's advantage is largest and where the inventory shows funding pull already in place.

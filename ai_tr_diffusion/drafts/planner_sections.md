## 1. Executive summary

This report scans 73 AI-driven developments (48 across all major sectors, 25 in ecology, research and civil society), scores each on six diffusion drivers, and classifies each for two horizons, 2026–2028 and 2028–2031, using a fixed rule rather than judgement. Evidence was gathered on 3 October 2026 from 676 sources, two independent Opus review passes challenged every classification, and the resulting data files accompany this report.

The headline is that AI is reaching Türkiye through three channels of very different reliability. The most reliable is binding regulation, and almost all of it is imported: the EU AI Act reaches Turkish exporters and EU-facing providers (A39, A29, A50), and EU-harmonised or sectoral rules do the same in medical devices and banking (A02, A45). No Turkish AI-systems law exists. The second channel is the state deploying where it owns both the data and the platform: tax-risk triage (A12), national crop monitoring (A14) and a student assistant with roughly 719,000 users (A08) already run at scale, while citizen-facing services that cut across agencies are mostly announcements (A03, A11, A32, A48). The third channel is funding, and it is what carries ecology: six biodiversity-monitoring developments are classed Likely (B01, B02, B03, B06, B17, B23), but four of them depend on a single Biodiversa+ call stream open to Turkish teams (S108), and operational use in Türkiye is still largely absent.

Of the 73 developments, 17 are Certain in both horizons, 8 are Likely in 2026–2028 rising to 16 in 2028–2031, and the rest are Potential. No development was classed low-probability; this reflects the rule, which required a sourced structural barrier, not proof that barriers do not exist. Half of the 2028–2031 Likely classes (8 of 16) rest on an assumed lag behind comparable economies rather than on Turkish evidence and should be read as indicative.

Two patterns matter most for a reader deciding what to do. First, governance arrives before deployment: rules for generative AI in research and universities are already in force (B14, A09) while the tools they govern are still Potential (B10, B11, B13, B25), so anything built now must fit existing disclosure and verification duties. Second, in 12 of the 17 open Tier B developments the deciding condition is not technology or data but a named Turkish institution funding a real deployment. Announcement volume overstates Turkish deployment: four developments were downgraded during review because only a label, an offline study or an unverified figure stood behind the "AI" claim (A47, A25, A27, A46).

For the researcher profile in Section 7 the synthesis recommends three directions, in order: AI-supported monitoring of wetland and delta restoration with the Gediz Delta as demonstrator; institution-facing evidence synthesis run on verified multi-agent pipelines; and municipal nature data combining citizen science with satellite mapping, with the metropolitan municipality as first client. The first step of each can be taken within 90 days and is specified in Section 6.

## 6. Synthesis

### 6.1 General landscape

The classification pattern says more about Türkiye than about AI. Technology and data are rarely the binding constraint: the infrastructure-and-data driver (d4) sits at 3 or 4 for most developments, and no row produced a sourced structural barrier. What separates Certain from Potential is almost always an institution. Where a binding rule exists, it was written in Brussels or copied from an EU framework; where a platform runs at national scale, a ministry owns the data; where a Likely class appears without Turkish deployment, a funding programme is doing the work. The state has shown it can move fast when it controls the whole stack (KURGAN went into production in October 2025; MEBİ's assistant reached hundreds of thousands of students within a year), and slowly when several agencies or external providers must agree.

Civil society follows the same logic one level down. Every documented civil-society deployment in the inventory is municipal (B20 Melikgazi, B19 İzmir staff training, B27 Bağcılar's İSTKA-funded twin, B26 call-centre routing), and the NGO side is barely visible in the evidence (B18 absent, B21 weak, B22 absent). This is not because NGOs lack the tools, which are cheap and often free, but because nobody has yet documented a funded, operational use that would count as evidence.

Ecology is the clearest case of funding-led diffusion. The six Likely monitoring developments owe their class to Biodiversa+ eligibility and to cost evidence from peer-reviewed work abroad, not to Turkish practice. The Türkiye baseline is a handful of research pilots (camera-trap detection at İYTE, eDNA surveys at Ankara University and Boğaziçi, a TRDizin paper on Aras bird calls) and one international training on the Gediz Delta (S501). The gap between "eligible and cheap" and "operational and funded" is exactly the space a connected researcher can occupy, and it is where the three directions below sit.

### 6.2 Three recommended directions

The researcher's advantages, as the inventory reveals them, are specific: field expertise in wetlands, herpetofauna, Odonata and vegetation that can produce the ground truth every Earth-observation model in Türkiye lacks; partners that already appear in the evidence (Tour du Valat's Mediterranean Wetlands Observatory trained Ege University and Doğa Derneği on the Gediz Delta, S501; the Gediz wet-meadow restoration monitors soil and water with no AI or satellite layer, S525); a municipal partner at a time when municipalities are the only civil-society actors deploying; and production-grade multi-agent Claude Code workflows that stand in for a coding team. The advice that follows from this is to position as the person who validates, operationalises and synthesises, not the one who builds models. Two tempting directions were rejected on that basis: corporate nature disclosure and biodiversity credits (B07, B24) depend on a binding duty the researcher cannot influence, and building EO foundation models or "AI co-scientist" systems (B05, B25) needs ML engineering capacity the profile does not have and adds less value than being their validated user.

**Direction 1. AI-supported monitoring of wetland and delta restoration, with the Gediz Delta as demonstrator.**
Why: digital measurement of restoration outcomes (B06) is Likely in both horizons and fits the Biodiversa+ call text better than any other item ("setting restoration targets and measuring success", S108). Drone habitat mapping (B23) and acoustic monitoring (B01) are Likely. Satellite wetland mapping (B04) is Potential, and its deciding condition, a funded operational wetland-monitoring service in Türkiye, is one the researcher's own network can create. Nobody currently runs a field-validated AI monitoring product for a Turkish delta.
When: 2026–2028 through consortium funding, triggered by a Biodiversa+ edition open to Turkish teams or a Horizon Europe Earth-observation topic (S514 lists Türkiye as eligible for the 2026 Cluster 4 EO topics, per search summary). In 2028–2031 it becomes an operational service if the site management body or the metropolitan municipality adopts it, which would move B04 to Certain.
First step, next 90 days: this week, check whether TÜBİTAK participates in the Biodiversa+ "Novel ecosystems" (BiodivFuture) call, launched 9 September 2026 with pre-proposals due in early November 2026 (S675, S676; participation unconfirmed at the time of writing). A delta reshaped by salt pans, drainage and restoration is a defensible novel ecosystem. If Türkiye participates, join or lead a pre-proposal with Tour du Valat as EU partner and the national nature NGO as stakeholder partner. Whatever the call outcome, build a 2017–2026 Sentinel-1/2 wetland-extent and condition time series for the Gediz Delta using MWO tooling or Google Earth Engine driven by a Claude Code pipeline, validate it against the researcher's own vegetation plots and Odonata and herpetofauna sites, and offer it to the wet-meadow restoration team as the missing outcome layer.
Main risk: single-call dependency. Four Likely classes rest on one reading of the Biodiversa+ call text, and Türkiye has no Nature Restoration Law duty (B09). Hedge by designing one indicator set that serves three buyers at once, namely national CBD/GBF and Ramsar reporting (B09), the municipality's adaptation plans (B27) and restoration MRV (B06), and by pursuing TÜBİTAK 1001 or a Horizon EO topic in parallel. The technical risk, class confusion among saltmarsh, reed and salt-pan habitats, is exactly what the researcher's field validation addresses, and that validation is the selling point.

**Direction 2. Institution-facing evidence synthesis run on verified multi-agent pipelines.**
Why: LLM-assisted screening and extraction (B11) has the strongest cost evidence in the inventory and is Potential only because analogue use was not detected. AI-maintained living evidence for conservation decisions (B12) is Potential with the deciding condition "a funded operational Turkish deployment", which a single funded pilot by the researcher would satisfy, moving it to Certain under the rule. Evidence-to-policy briefs (B22) need Turkish-language validation. The governance frame is already fixed (B14 Certain; TÜBİTAK and YÖK disclosure and verification rules, S100, S101, S416), so the compliance cost of starting now is known. This report is itself a worked example of the method.
When: now. This direction does not wait for diffusion because the researcher is the adopter. Scale depends on a public client with funding, for example the next TÜBİTAK 1007 Kamu YZ Ekosistemi edition, whose 2024 and 2026 rounds both carried a climate and sustainability theme (S588, S589) but require a public customer institution; Sivil Düşün (S582) can fund the NGO or cooperative side.
First step, next 90 days: deliver one demonstrator synthesis for a named partner, WWF-Türkiye or the national nature NGO, on a question they already have, such as the effectiveness of wetland and wet-meadow restoration interventions in Mediterranean deltas or amphibian and Odonata responses to pond creation and hydrological restoration. Write it to Conservation Evidence conventions with a Turkish policy brief. Build in a measured verification protocol, a manual double-check of at least 20 percent of screening and extraction decisions with reported accuracy and hours saved, and publish that protocol as a short methods note. This creates the Turkish evidence for B11 and B12 that the scan could not find.
Main risk: commoditisation, since Elicit, Consensus and Scopus AI are cheap and spreading (B10). Hedge by selling domain judgement, Turkish-language output and an audit trail rather than search. The second risk is credibility loss from fabricated citations or extraction errors (B15). Hedge with human-verified extraction, a disclosed AI-use statement under the TÜBİTAK and YÖK rules, and one review of the pipeline by a co-author who reads code.

**Direction 3. Municipal nature data combining citizen science and satellite mapping, with the metropolitan municipality as first client and the cooperative as operator.**
Why: AI-assisted citizen-science species identification (B17) is Likely in both horizons, yet Turkish activity is event-scale (S451). Expert validators for difficult taxa are the bottleneck automated identification cannot remove, and Odonata and herpetofauna are exactly such taxa. Urban-nature and climate-adaptation mapping for municipalities (B27) is Potential now and Likely later, with a deciding condition that names the researcher's partner type: a funded metropolitan project that operationalises canopy, heat or blue-green mapping. Municipalities are training staff but lack operational tools (B19, B26).
When: 2026–2028 for the citizen-science layer, which needs no new funding; 2028–2031 for an operational municipal service. Triggers are a municipal adaptation or EU Cities Mission commitment (İzmir is a Cities Mission city, S591, if the partner is İzmir Büyükşehir Belediyesi), the next TÜBİTAK 1007 edition, or a development-agency AI programme on the İSTKA model that funded Bağcılar at 90 percent (S593); an equivalent İZKA programme is an unsourced estimate.
First step, next 90 days: agree with the municipality's environment or climate department a one-season urban blue-green biodiversity baseline for three to five urban wetlands, streams or parks. The cooperative runs public iNaturalist bioblitz events under one umbrella project with the researcher as expert validator for Odonata and herpetofauna; pair the observations with a Sentinel-2 change map of green and blue cover built in the same pipeline as Direction 1; and sign a short data protocol with an open licence and municipal use rights before the first event.
Main risk: municipal budget and political cycles, and dependence on a single department. Hedge with a memorandum that includes an open-data clause so the dataset survives staff turnover, and a co-applicant NGO so the work can move to Sivil Düşün or Biodiversa+ funding. The accuracy of automated identification for Turkish Odonata and herpetofauna is a second risk; reporting expert-validated accuracy turns it into a publishable result.

### 6.3 How the three fit together

The three directions share one pipeline and one validation asset. The Sentinel time series built for the Gediz Delta in Direction 1 is the same change map Direction 3 needs for urban blue-green cover; the verification protocol of Direction 2 is what makes the monitoring outputs of Directions 1 and 3 citable by institutions. Direction 2 can start this month with no external funding and produces the first Turkish deployment evidence; Direction 1 is the funding play and should be timed to the Biodiversa+ pre-proposal window; Direction 3 is the lowest-cost, highest-visibility local anchor. If only one can be pursued, Direction 1 is the choice, because it is where the researcher's advantage is largest and where the inventory shows funding pull already in place.

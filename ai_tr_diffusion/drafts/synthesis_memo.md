## 1. General landscape (≤300 words)

AI reaches Türkiye through three channels. The pattern of the classes shows which channel carries which kind of development.

**Binding rules, mostly imported.** Five of the 17 Certain items rest on an obligation (d1 ≥ 4), none of them a Turkish AI law. The EU AI Act reaches Turkish exporters and EU-facing providers (A39, A29, A50). EU-harmonised or sectoral rules do the same (A02 MDR-aligned devices, A45 BDDK liveness). Türkiye has no AI-systems law, only a deepfake bill (S090). Two more Certain items, A09 and B14, are rules in place, not technology in use.

**Operational public platforms.** The state deploys where it owns the data and the platform: KURGAN tax triage (A12), Tarım Ürün İzleme Sistemi (A14), MEB MEBİ/KANKA with about 719k students (A08). Citizen-facing, multi-agency platforms are still only announced (A03, A11, A32, A48).

**Funding channels.** These create Likely classes without Turkish deployment: Biodiversa+ via TÜBİTAK 1071 (S108) carries B01, B06, B17 and B23, and the sectoral Turkish-LLM call carries A40. TÜBİTAK 1007 (S588), Horizon Europe and Sivil Düşün lift many items only to d2 = 3, which alone does not give Likely.

**Why Tier B sits in Potential (17 of 25).** Technology and data are rarely the constraint (d4 is 3–4; no barrier sourced). In 12 of 17 rows the deciding condition is a named Turkish institution moving to a funded operational deployment (B04, B10, B12, B13, B15, B18, B19, B21, B22, B25, B26, B27). The rest need a binding duty (B07, B24), a call naming the area (B05, B09) or analogue evidence not found (B11). The deciding variable is an institutional adopter with a budget, a lever a well-connected researcher can pull.

## 2. Cross-cutting patterns (each anchored on ≥2 ids)

- **Regulation reaches Türkiye through exporters and harmonisation, not through domestic AI law.** The d1 = 4 routes are EU extraterritorial reach (A39, A29, A50) or EU-aligned or sectoral secondary rules (A02, A45). Where the EU rule touches only EU-market providers, the pull stays at 3 and the item stays Potential (A51 Annex III). B07 shows the same at the corporate end: TSRS is mandatory but has no nature-specific duty, so nature disclosure stays Potential.
- **Governance arrives before deployment.** Rules on generative AI in research and teaching are Certain (B14, A09), while the tools they govern are Potential (B10, B11, B13, B25) or only recently Certain via survey use (A10). The same order holds for the AI Act obligations (A39, A50) and the HR uses they will eventually cover (A51). For the reader this means the compliance frame (disclosure, verification, no AI in evaluation per TÜBİTAK S100/S101) is already fixed. Tools adopted later must fit it.
- **The state adopts first where it controls data and platform. Civil society adopts first where a municipality does.** The central platforms are A12, A14 and A08. In Tier B civil society, the only Certain item is municipal (B20, Melikgazi MEGA). Every other civil-society signal of activity is also municipal: İzmir BB training 428 staff and the Nilüfer AI office (B19), Bağcılar's İSTKA-funded digital twin (B27), and ALO 153 routing (B26, A11). The NGO side is barely documented (B18 absent, B21 weak, B22 absent).
- **Analogue lag is real but measured in intensity more often than in arrival.** Few dated pairs exist. For municipal chatbots, Mexico (2020) and Seoul (2022) precede Melikgazi (2025), roughly 3–5 years (B20). In the same 2024 TALIS round, 24% of Turkish teachers used AI, against 43% in Korea and 56% in Brazil (A10). For governance there is no lag: YÖK 2024 sits before Poland's NCN 2025 and Brazil's CNPq 2026 (B14). Half of the 3–5y Likely classes (8 of 16: A11, A16, A32, A41, A42, A44, A46, B27) rest on the assumed lag, not on sourced pull. The report should not treat these as forecasts.
- **Ecology is funding-led, not adoption-led.** Six Tier B ecology items are Likely (B01, B02, B03, B06, B17, B23), yet the Türkiye status of most of them is "absent" or "research pilot". Four reach Likely through a single call reading (S108, review_log Q5). The continuation of Biodiversa+ editions open to TR is therefore the most important single condition in Tier B.
- **Turkish language is a validation requirement, not a barrier.** No row sources a language barrier, and the Turkish-LLM programme is Likely (A40, sectoral LLM call). Yet deciding conditions repeatedly require "Turkish-language validation" before a pilot counts (A01, A03, A11, B22, B26). Validated Turkish-language performance on a real institutional corpus is the scarce input, and producing it is a contribution in its own right.
- **Production evidence, not announcements, separates Certain from Potential.** Rows were downgraded where only a label, an announcement or an offline study existed: A47 "AI-supported" irrigation, A25 the Getir offline RL study, A27 an unverified survey figure, A46 a single self-report. Expect many Turkish "AI" announcements to fail this test. The report can say plainly that announcement volume overstates Turkish deployment.

## 3. Recommended directions for the §7 profile (exactly three, ranked)

The researcher's unfair advantages in this inventory are four. First, a field ecologist with wetland, delta, herpetofauna, Odonata and vegetation expertise who can produce ground truth, which every EO/AI monitoring item lacks in Türkiye. Second, partners that already appear in the evidence: S501 records the Mediterranean Wetlands Observatory (MWO, hosted by Tour du Valat) training Ege University and Doğa Derneği on the Gediz Delta, and S525 records the Gediz wet-meadow restoration, which monitors groundwater and soil without any AI or EO component. Third, a municipal partner. Fourth, production-grade multi-agent Claude Code workflows, which substitute for a coding team. The career advice is to position as the person who validates, operationalises and synthesises, not the one who builds models.

### Direction 1: AI-supported monitoring of wetland and delta restoration, with the Gediz Delta as demonstrator
- **Why:** B06 (digital MRV of restoration outcomes) is Likely/Likely, and its call fit is the best of any item (S108 Topic 1, "setting restoration targets and measuring success"). B23 (drone habitat mapping, deep learning) and B01 (acoustic monitoring) are Likely/Likely. B04 (deep-learning wetland mapping from Sentinel-1/2) is Potential/Potential. B04's deciding condition is "a funded operational wetland-monitoring service in Türkiye (ministry or Gediz Delta management body)", which the researcher's own network can create. B09, reworded to national CBD/GBF reporting, is Potential (near-Likely). The Turkish baseline is research groups plus one MWO training (S401, S501). Nobody yet runs an operational, field-validated AI monitoring product for a Turkish delta.
- **When:** 1–2y via consortium funding. The trigger is a Biodiversa+ edition open to TR, or an EO-themed Horizon call (S514 lists TR as eligible for CL4-2026 EO topics; per summary). 3–5y as an operational service if the site manager (DKMP/Ramsar site management) or the metropolitan municipality adopts it. That would move B04 to Certain via the C1 third clause.
- **First step (next 90 days):**
  1. This week, check whether TÜBİTAK participates in Biodiversa+ BiodivFuture (novel ecosystems; pre-proposals early Nov 2026). A delta reshaped by salt pans, drainage and restoration is a defensible "novel ecosystem" with trajectories that can be measured by EO time series.
  2. If TR participates, join or lead a pre-proposal with Tour du Valat as the EU partner and the national nature NGO as the stakeholder partner.
  3. Whatever the call outcome, produce a Gediz Delta wetland-extent and condition time series for 2017–2026 with Sentinel-1/2. Use MWO tooling (GEOclassifier) or Google Earth Engine, driven by a Claude Code multi-agent pipeline. Validate it against the researcher's own vegetation plots and Odonata and herpetofauna sites.
  4. Offer it to the wet-meadow restoration team (S525) as the missing outcome layer.
- **Main risk and hedge:**
  - **Single-call dependency.** Four Likely classes rest on one reading of S108 (Q5), and Türkiye has no NRR duty (B09). Hedge: design one indicator set that serves three buyers: CBD/GBF and Ramsar national reporting (B09), the municipality's adaptation plans (B27), and restoration MRV (B06). Pursue two channels in parallel: Biodiversa+, plus TÜBİTAK 1001 or a Horizon EO topic.
  - **Technical risk:** deep-learning class confusion in heterogeneous delta habitats such as saltmarsh, reed and salt pans. The researcher's field validation is the hedge, and it is the selling point.

### Direction 2: Institution-facing evidence synthesis run on verified multi-agent LLM pipelines
- **Why:** B11 (LLM-assisted screening and extraction) has d3 = 4, well sourced, and is Potential only because analogue use was not detected. B12 (AI-maintained living evidence for conservation decisions) is Potential with deciding condition "a funded operational Turkish deployment (university, NGO or municipality)". A single funded pilot by the researcher would satisfy it (d6 3 + funded + d2 3 → Certain via the C1 third clause). B22 (evidence-to-policy briefs) is Potential and needs Turkish-language validation. B14 (Certain) means the rules on disclosure and verification already apply. This study is itself a worked example of the method.
- **When:** now, at the 1–2y horizon. This direction does not wait for diffusion, because the researcher can be the adopter. The trigger for scale is a public client with funding, for example the next TÜBİTAK 1007 Kamu YZ Ekosistemi edition (2024 and 2026 editions both had a "Climate Change and Sustainability" theme, S588/S589; it needs a public customer institution, so check whether a metropolitan municipality qualifies). Sivil Düşün (S582) can fund the NGO or cooperative side.
- **First step (next 90 days):**
  1. Deliver one demonstrator synthesis for a named partner, for example for WWF-Türkiye or the national nature NGO: "Effectiveness of wetland and wet-meadow restoration interventions in Mediterranean deltas", or "Amphibian and Odonata responses to pond creation and hydrological restoration".
  2. Write it to Conservation Evidence conventions, with a Turkish policy brief.
  3. Build in a measured verification protocol: a manual double-check of a ≥20% sample of screening and extraction decisions, reporting accuracy and hours saved.
  4. Publish the protocol as a short methods note. This also creates the Turkish evidence for B11 and B12 that the scan could not find.
- **Main risk and hedge:**
  - **Commoditisation.** Elicit, Consensus and Scopus AI are cheap and spreading (B10). Hedge: sell domain judgement, Turkish-language output and an audit trail, not search.
  - **Credibility risk** from fabricated citations or extraction errors (B15, B14 rules). Hedge: human-verified extraction and a disclosed AI-use statement per TÜBİTAK S100/S101 and YÖK S416. Have a co-author with coding literacy review the pipeline once (the researcher does not read code).

### Direction 3: Municipal nature data, combining citizen science and EO, with the metropolitan municipality as first client and the cooperative as operator
- **Why:** B17 (AI-assisted citizen-science species ID) is Likely/Likely, but the Turkish activity is event-scale (Tür Say, 1,484 observations; S451). Expert identifiers for difficult taxa are the bottleneck that automated ID cannot remove, and Odonata and herpetofauna are exactly such taxa. B27 (urban-nature and climate-adaptation mapping for municipalities) is Potential / Likely (lag-based). Its deciding condition, "a funded metropolitan-municipality project that operationalises canopy, heat or blue-green mapping", names the researcher's partner type. B19 and B26 (Potential) show that municipalities are training staff (İzmir BB, 428 staff, S462, weak source) but lack operational tools. Municipalities are the most active civil-society adopters (pattern above).
- **When:** 1–2y for the citizen-science layer, which needs no new funding. 3–5y for an operational municipal service. Triggers: a municipal adaptation or Cities-Mission commitment (İzmir is an EU Cities Mission city, S591, if the partner is İzmir BB), the next TÜBİTAK 1007 Kamu YZ edition, or a development-agency AI programme. The İSTKA precedent is the 90% grant for Bağcılar (S593). An equivalent programme at İZKA is an unsourced estimate.
- **First step (next 90 days):**
  1. With the municipality's environment or climate department, agree a one-season "urban blue-green biodiversity baseline" for 3–5 urban wetlands, streams or parks.
  2. The cooperative runs public iNaturalist bioblitz events under one umbrella project, and the researcher acts as expert validator for Odonata and herpetofauna.
  3. Pair the observations with a Sentinel-2 change map of green and blue cover, built in the same Claude Code pipeline as Direction 1.
  4. Sign a short data protocol (open licence, municipal use rights) before the first event.
- **Main risk and hedge:**
  - **Municipal budget and political cycles,** and dependence on one department. Hedge: an MoU with an open-data clause, so the dataset survives staff turnover, plus a co-applicant NGO so the work can move to Sivil Düşün or Biodiversa+ funding.
  - **Accuracy of automated ID for Turkish Odonata and herpetofauna.** Report expert-validated accuracy. That turns the weakness into a publishable result.

## 5. Candidate headline findings (executive summary)
1. Of 73 AI developments, 17 are classed Certain to be present in Türkiye in both 2026–28 and 2028–31. Likely items double from 8 to 16 between the horizons, and no item is classed low-probability, because no structural barrier was sourced (all ids; Low = 0).
2. Binding pull on Turkish actors currently comes from EU law and EU-aligned or sectoral rules, not from a Turkish AI law. This applies to exporters and EU-facing providers (A39, A29, A50) and to regulated sectors (A02, A45).
3. AI is already operational at national scale where the state owns both the data and the platform: tax-risk triage (A12), crop monitoring (A14) and student assistants (A08). Citizen-facing services across agencies are mostly at the announcement stage (A03, A11, A32, A48).
4. Rules for AI in research and education are in place before the tools are widely used (B14, A09 versus B10, B11, B13). New tools will have to fit existing disclosure and verification duties.
5. In ecology, AI monitoring is classed Likely mainly because of funding pull, notably a Biodiversa+ call open to Turkish teams (B01, B06, B17, B23; S108). Operational use in Türkiye is still largely absent, so the classes depend on that call stream continuing.
6. For 17 of 25 deep-focus developments the outcome is still open. In most cases the missing piece is a Turkish institution funding a real deployment, not technology or data (B04, B12, B19, B27 and others).
7. Municipalities are the most active civil-society adopters (B20, B19, B27). Documented AI use by NGOs is rare (B18, B21, B22).
8. Half of the Likely classes for 2028–31 (8 of 16) rest on an assumed time lag behind comparable countries rather than on Turkish evidence (A11, A16, A32, A41, A42, A44, A46, B27). Treat them as indicative.

## 6. Limitations the report must state (§2)
- **Search-only sourcing.** Evidence comes from web-search results and Consensus/Scholar abstracts. A session cap of 200 searches degraded several batches (b1, b3–b9), which were partly re-run. Turkish grey literature is under-detected, so d6 = 1–2 and analogue_count = 0 often mean "not found", not "absent" (explicitly B11, B13, A18).
- **No HTTP fetch.** Outbound HTTP was blocked. URLs count as verified when they appeared in search results or passed a structural or DOI-format check (step 2h: 590 format_ok). Pages were not opened.
- **Weak sources.** 73 source rows are secondary, vendor or search-summary items. They were kept and labelled "weak", and no class rests on a weak source alone after review. Vendor claims cap d3 at 3.
- **Abstracts-only reading,** by user decision. Peer-reviewed work gives direction and corroboration and was not appraised in full.
- **Analogue-lag assumption.** The 3–5y class adds +1 to d6 when ≥2 analogues exist, and Likely reached only this way is labelled "Likely (lag-based)" (8 items). The lag is assumed, not measured. Analogue counting was uneven (plans and trainings were excluded after review, and some affiliations were inferred: B23, B27).
- **S108 scope reading.** d2 = 4 for monitoring items (B01, B04, B06, B17, B23) rests on reading the 2025–26 BiodivConnect call, which names no technology, as covering items whose core activity is monitoring (planner Q5). Under a strict reading, B01, B17 and B23 would be Potential (near-Likely). Whether TÜBİTAK takes part in the 2026–27 BiodivFuture call is unverified.
- **Survey-share rule.** A primary national survey showing ≥10% use counts as d6 = 4 (Q3). This lifted A10 (TALIS 2024, 24%; the figure came via a search summary of the OECD page). A27 was reverted because its 14% figure could not be verified on the primary page.
- **Rule mechanics.** The class follows mechanically from scores with fixed thresholds. Boundary cases (A21 and B25 at mean(d4,d5) = 2.0) can flip on one point, and the Low group is empty partly because barriers needed a source. Governance items (A09, A29, A39, A45, B14) are "Certain" as rules in force, not as deployed technology.
- **Secondary dating of EU rules.** AI Act Omnibus dates (A29, A39, A50, A51) come from law-firm and secondary notices, not from Official Journal text.
- **Scope.** Tourism was excluded (not in brief §2). The study is a snapshot at 2026-10-03 and is not an academic systematic review.

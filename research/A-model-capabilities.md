## Failure modes and what mitigates them
- Citation fabrication and wrong-field references: closed-book generation gives low existence rates and many metadata errors (#21-#23, #29); no Claude 5.x rate measured. Mitigate: never take references from memory; retrieve via PubMed/Scholar/DOI tools, resolve every DOI, check each claim against a quoted passage.
- Abstract-only reading: weakly evidenced for 5.x (#31). Mitigate: supply full text (1M window on Fable/Opus/Sonnet, not Haiku, #4) and require page-anchored quotes.
- Conservative or generic ideas: LLM ideation lacks diversity and self-ranking fails (#24). Mitigate: sample many, deduplicate, rank by a human or a separate rubric, not the generating model.
- Shortcut-taking and early stops in long tasks (#15, #16): use a written checklist plus completion criterion, cap automatic continuations at 2-3, tell the model to open all sources first, avoid tight time budgets on verification steps.
- Accepting false premises or supplied records (#25, #32; sycophancy-adjacent, 5.x not measured): ask it to audit supplied claims against sources, run a separate adversarial review pass, and skip the "treat earlier answers as done" prompt (#17).
- Confident errors even as accuracy rises (#26, #27): allow and reward "unknown", require evidence for each factual claim.
- Safeguard refusals on life-science content or "show your reasoning" prompts (#18): ask for a short explanation instead, use server-side fallback, or apply for the verification program.
- Benchmark non-transfer: Anthropic itself says margins mislead (#11) and most headline numbers are coding (#10, #14) or agentic office work (#10); validate on your own tasks.

## Implications for a paper-writing workflow
- Treat all model-written references as untrusted: ground every citation in retrieved records (DOI/PubMed) and verify existence, metadata and claim support (#21-#23).
- Route by task, escalate on failure: Sonnet 5.5 for bulk reading/extraction, Opus 5.5 (set effort explicitly, start medium, raise for synthesis) for drafting, Fable 5.1 only when Opus fails your acceptance checks; avoid Haiku 4.5 for anything needing 2025-26 knowledge (#4, #5, #8).
- Feed full texts, state a checklist and completion criterion, and add a separate critique pass that must find errors, since 5.x sycophancy and early-stop behaviour are only vendor-described (#15, #16, #32).
- Control cost with caching (cache reads $0.20-0.25/MTok on the top three tiers) and Batch for non-urgent jobs; measure tokens per finished task rather than list price (#6, #26).
- Build a 10-document in-domain mini-eval (fabricated refs, claim support, instruction following, pushback on a planted false premise) before relying on vendor or coding benchmarks; check data-retention terms before sending unpublished work to Fable 5.1 (#14, #19).
